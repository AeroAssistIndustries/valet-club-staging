    In menswear now

    Updated November 2026

    • Slimmer tailoring is back on the runway

      Fall 2026 collections brought narrower cuts and sharper proportions back into view, with Prada and Saint Laurent leading the way.

      Our take: You don’t need new suits. A good tailor can taper what you own, and that’s the first thing we look at in a Closet Audit.

      Source: The Wed

    • Winter is preppy, with a sporty twist

      This season mixes classic tailoring and coats with practical touches such as toggles, hoods and extra pockets.

      Our take: Easy to wear in Arizona: an oxford shirt and blazer over something relaxed looks current without trying hard.

      Source: Wallpaper*, 2 November 2026

    • Color returns to cold-weather dressing

      Designers broke up the usual dark palette with teal, purple and other bright accents for fall and winter.

      Our take: Start small. One colored knit or a pocket square against navy or grey does the job.

      Source: Out

    • The tie isn’t going anywhere

      Ties stayed on the fall 2026 runways, worn loose, tucked in, or layered under knitwear.

      Our take: A knit tie with an open-weave blazer is the relaxed version. Keep it for dinners and events.

      Source: Coveteur

    • Looking ahead: spring 2027 goes colorful and collegiate

      Next spring’s shows in Paris and Milan favored sorbet-colored polos, lighter tailoring and preppy classics with a playful edge.

      Our take: Good news for the desert. Light polos and unlined jackets are exactly what works here from March on.

      Source: Outlook Luxe, 25 July 2026

    • Also for spring: clothes built for travel

      Travel-ready wardrobes and lighter layers ran through the spring 2027 menswear collections.

      Our take: If you’re on a plane twice a month, tell us. We’ll build looks that pack flat and mix across a week.

      Source: The Impression

    Dressing well in the desert

    Phoenix and Scottsdale ask more of a wardrobe than most cities. Five rules we use for Arizona clients.

    • Pick cloth that breathes. Linen, cotton-linen blends, seersucker and lightweight wool let heat out.
    • Go lighter in color. Stone, tan, light blue and off-white stay cooler in the sun than navy and black.
    • Choose unlined jackets. An unstructured blazer gives you the polish without the insulation.
    • Keep one layer handy. Offices and restaurants run cold. A fine knit over the shoulders solves it.
    • Swap boots for loafers. Loafers with no-show socks keep a tailored look comfortable in the heat.

    Style notes we give every client

    Fit comes first

    The shoulder seam should sit at the edge of your shoulder. Show a little shirt cuff past the jacket sleeve. Trousers should just touch the shoe.

    Build around three colors

    Navy, grey and one that’s yours, such as olive, brown or burgundy. When most pieces share a palette, everything goes with everything.

    A tailor is the best-value upgrade

    Hemming trousers, slimming a sleeve or taking in a waist costs little and makes mid-priced clothes look expensive.

    Spend where you wear

    Put the money into shoes, a jacket and a watch you’ll wear weekly. Think cost per wear, not price tag.

    One point of interest

    A textured jacket, a patterned shirt or a strong shoe. Pick one per outfit and keep the rest quiet.

    Match your leathers

    Belt and shoes should be close in color and finish. Brown with brown, black with black.

    Dress for the room, plus one

    Work out what most people will wear, then go one step sharper. You’ll never be the one underdressed.

    Look after what you own

    Wooden hangers for jackets, shoe trees for leather shoes, and a steamer between wears keep clothes looking new for years.

    Which service should I start with?

    If you’d like to get more from what you already own, start with The Closet Audit. If you know you need new outfits, The Men’s Club gets you 3–5 complete looks. If you want everything handled, including an audit, 5–7 looks, accessories and a final fit session, choose The Exclusive. A discovery call will help you decide.

    Do I have to buy a certain amount of clothing?

    No. There’s no clothing minimum and no pressure to purchase. We make recommendations, and you buy only what you love.

    Do you mark up the clothes you recommend?

    No. There’s no markup. Your service fee covers our time and expertise, and the clothes cost what the store charges.

    What happens on the discovery call?

    It’s a private conversation about your wardrobe, your goals, and which service is the right fit. You can pick a time online from our calendar.

    How long does a Closet Audit take?

    One to two hours. You’ll come away with a full closet review, new outfits from your existing wardrobe, a gap analysis, and recommendations for key pieces.
